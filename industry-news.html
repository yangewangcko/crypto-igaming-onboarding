Regulation · EU

MiCA authorisation requirement for crypto merchants

Crypto

The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) introduces a single licensing regime for crypto-asset businesses across the EEA.

MiCA is the European Union's comprehensive framework for regulating crypto-assets and the firms that service them. It replaces the previous patchwork of national regimes with one harmonised set of rules.

What's changing

  • Rules for stablecoins — asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens (EMTs) — applied from 30 June 2024.
  • Authorisation and conduct requirements for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) applied from 30 December 2024.
  • A transitional ("grandfathering") window lets firms already operating under national law continue until they are authorised — up to 1 July 2026, though individual member states may shorten this.

What it means for you

If you offer crypto services to customers in the EEA, you will need to hold — or be actively pursuing — CASP authorisation from a national competent authority. Expect authorisation status, licensed entity, and regulated jurisdictions to form part of onboarding due diligence. Have your licence details ready when you apply. See Preparation for the documents to gather.

Card schemes · Global

Account Funding Transaction (AFT) mandate

Crypto & iGaming

Visa and Mastercard require payments that fund an account or wallet to be identified as Account Funding Transactions and carry enhanced sender and recipient data.

An Account Funding Transaction (AFT) is a card payment whose purpose is to load funds into an account, wallet, or balance — for example buying crypto, topping up an e-wallet, or making a gambling deposit. The card schemes now require these to be flagged and enriched so the money movement is transparent.

What's changing

  • Transactions in affected categories — including MCC 6051 (quasi-cash / crypto) and MCC 7995 (gambling) — must be submitted as AFTs rather than standard purchases.
  • Merchants must pass additional data on the request, including the sender's name, address, and funding account reference, plus recipient details where required.
  • Both schemes are rolling the requirement out in stages; incomplete or mis-flagged transactions risk higher decline rates and non-compliance fees.

What it means for you

Make sure your integration flags qualifying payments as AFTs and supplies the mandated data fields. Checkout.com supports AFT processing — confirming these fields during integration protects your authorisation rates.
